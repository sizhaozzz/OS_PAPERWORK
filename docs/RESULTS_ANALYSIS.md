# Results analysis, discrepancy assessment and reporting statements

This document complements the README. It turns the archived experiment CSVs
into a Paper-vs-Ours comparison, gives each claim a verdict, quantifies every
discrepancy, and records the limitations and reporting statements required by
the course. Every number below is traceable to a CSV under `results/` and, from
there, to the per-run logs in the same bundle.

## 2. Analysis of discrepancies

Every difference from the paper is quantified and explained below. Explanations
are based on the recorded settings and controlled checks, not speculation.

| # | Discrepancy | Absolute / relative gap | Evidence and explanation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | reorder_5 Serial total schedules | -471.67 schedules, -9.4% (4,568.33 vs 5,040) | Repetition 3 completed all 5,040; reps 1-2 hit the 1,800 s wrapper timeout at 4,142 and 4,523 schedules. See `experiments.log` and the three `reorder_5-Serial-rep*.log` files. Not a bug-count discrepancy: all 3 reps found the first failure at schedule 283. Raising `--timeout` is the controlled re-run that would close this gap. |
| 2 | reorder_10 Serial | capped at 10,001 vs paper ">10,000" | Consistent with the paper's upper-bound statement; the run was capped by design, not a divergence. 0/3 bug found matches the paper's implication. |
| 3 | Number of repetitions | 3 vs 10 in the paper | Reduced scale to bound runtime (stated scope). Mean and stdev over 3 runs are still reported for every configuration. |
| 4 | Hardware | i7-14650HX laptop vs Xeon Silver 4214 workstation | Recorded in `environment.log`. Affects wall-clock time and timing-dependent dynamic prefix feedback; explains the stdev on CVEs with deep prefix expansion. |
| 5 | OS/kernel | Ubuntu 18.04.5 in Docker on WSL2 vs native Ubuntu 18.04 | Recorded in `environment.log`. SCHED_DEADLINE probe passes; kernel timing is not identical, the source of the modest run-to-run variation. |
| 6 | Workload coverage | 10 CVEs + 4 reorder vs 10 CVEs + 36 SCTBench | Scope decision: C1/C2 target the two selected claims only. Other SCTBench programs, competing tools, runtime-overhead (Figure 7) and new-bug discovery are out of scope. |
| 7 | Paper Section 4.2 four-thread Serial value | One sentence disagrees with Table 2 | We follow Table 2 (384), which our run matches exactly. The discrepancy exists in the paper itself. |
| 8 | C1 total-schedule counts | No paper reference value | Table 1 does not publish total schedules; ours is an added measurement, so no gap can be computed. |

No discrepancy changes a claim verdict. The only quantitative gaps (#1, #2) are
fully accounted for by the declared timeout and budget, and #1 can be reduced by
a longer timeout in a follow-up run.

## 3. Limitations

- Results were obtained on one hardware/OS combination; they do not establish
  portability across kernels or CPU architectures.
- PERIOD's static key-point identification is over-approximated via SVF; missed
  key points could hide bugs (a limitation the paper also acknowledges).
- Program inputs are fixed as compiled; no test-input generation is explored.
- Weak memory models and probabilistic programs are not handled.
- The depth-bound extension was executed on a single program (`reorder_5`) with
  3 repetitions per depth; the saturation for `d >= 3` is specific to this
  bug's effective depth and should not be generalised to other workloads. See
  `docs/EXTENSION.md` for the full measurement table and interpretation.
- RQ2–RQ4 (broader bug finding, d×period grid, SCHED_DEADLINE overhead) and the
  four presentation Critical-Analysis points are reported separately in
  `docs/RQ_ANALYSIS.md`. In particular, PCT/IPB/Maple are absent from the
  released image, so the RQ2 comparison uses the paper's published numbers as
  the external reference.
